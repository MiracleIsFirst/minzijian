Research

Reliable learning for visual restoration.

My work studies how robust estimation, generative priors, and learning-based optimization can be combined to solve difficult visual inverse problems under uncertainty, outliers, and imperfect models.

01 · Robust Image Restoration

I study restoration methods that remain effective when observations contain outliers, model mismatch, non-Gaussian corruption, or unreliable measurements. A recurring idea is to reason about consensus: instead of trusting every observation or every generated hypothesis equally, identify the subset that agrees with a structurally plausible solution.

This line began with robust geometric fitting and blind deblurring, including maximum-consensus formulations for handling corrupted image evidence.

02 · Generative Restoration

I extend robust estimation to modern generative priors, especially diffusion and flow-based models. My recent work investigates how posterior sampling can remain reliable when the forward degradation model is noisy or partially misspecified.

The long-term goal is to combine the expressive prior of pretrained generative models with explicit mechanisms for structural fidelity, robustness, and controllable restoration.

03 · Learning to Optimize Restoration

I am interested in reinforcement learning and test-time optimization for image restoration. Rather than using a generative model as a fixed prior, I study how it can adapt online to image-quality verifiers, restoration success signals, and input-specific constraints while preserving the pretrained model's useful distribution.

This direction connects maximum-consensus reasoning with policy optimization: generate diverse hypotheses, evaluate which trajectories satisfy restoration objectives, and reinforce high-quality solutions.

04 · Multimodal Representation Learning

I also work on multimodal learning, including multimodal emotion recognition in conversations. This work studies how graph structure, temporal dynamics, state-space modeling, and cross-modal interaction can represent evolving conversational states.
